# Policy and Standards Gap Mapping

The gaps below are domain-level findings for child-facing generative AI. They are not country-specific legal conclusions.

| Gap | Evidence from mapping | Global example | Control demonstrated |
|---|---|---|---|
| Guardian approval before generated content reaches a child | High-level child safety and responsible AI principles do not define one operational approval point | UNICEF caregiver controls UK Children's Code parental support and COPPA parental consent practices | Mandatory guardian-review action before the child-facing sink |
| Testable Arabic age appropriateness | Age-appropriate principles exist but do not provide a common Arabic language test for vocabulary tone and cultural context | UK Children's Code age-appropriate application and UNICEF child-centred design | Age band simple Arabic topic restriction and fixed safe replies |
| Treatment of inferred child data sent to model providers | Direct identifiers can be minimized while sensitive interests or vulnerability are still inferred | UNICEF privacy and well-being guidance plus EU AI Act age-vulnerability approach | Local offline mode purpose classification and provider withholding for blocked uses |
| Commercial targeting from inferred vulnerability | General privacy and welfare rules need a concrete product test for targeted offers | EU AI Act prohibition approach for harmful exploitation of age-related vulnerability | `commercial_targeting` classification and fail-closed escalation |
| Human supervision as a decision barrier | Human oversight can be described without specifying where it prevents delivery | ITU readiness human-supervision perspective | Guardian approval placed between distribution and the child-facing sink |

## Proposed standards contribution

A future domain profile could define a child-facing AI delivery gate with five testable requirements: declared audience, minimized context, source-grounded policy check, guardian decision status, and a structured audit event. Arabic age-appropriateness benchmarks and treatment of inferred child data require further multi-stakeholder work.
