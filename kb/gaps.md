# Domain Policy and Standards Gaps

## Guardian approval for generated child content

The mapping does not identify a single operational standard that defines when a guardian must approve generative-AI content before it reaches a child. The demo therefore makes guardian review a mandatory control and compares it with caregiver-control practices in UNICEF guidance, the UK Children's Code, and COPPA.

## Testable Arabic age appropriateness

High-level principles support age-appropriate content, but a testable benchmark for Arabic vocabulary, tone, cultural context, and developmental suitability remains difficult to identify. The demo constrains audience, age band, length, and topics, then records the applied control.

## Inferred child data and external model providers

Systems may infer interests, emotional state, or vulnerability even when direct identifiers are removed. The mapping needs clearer operational treatment of these inferences, provider transfer, retention, and secondary use. The demo prohibits commercial secondary use and keeps a local offline path.

## Commercial targeting based on vulnerability

General privacy and child-welfare rules do not by themselves provide a complete technical test for offers derived from a child's inferred weakness or behaviour. The demo classifies and blocks this use, using the EU AI Act's age-vulnerability approach as a global comparison.

## Human supervision in the machine learning pipeline

The pipeline needs an explicit decision barrier showing that human supervision is more than observation. Mobdeen Guardian places guardian approval between distribution and the child-facing sink.
